# News Style

## Overview

Generate original local-news copy in the style of pro.berdyansk.biz: practical, city-focused, fact-led, source-attributed, and written for residents who want to know what happened, where, when, who is responsible, and what changes for them.

Do not copy source articles or reuse distinctive phrasing from them. Use this as a style guide for new material only.

## Core Voice

- Write as a local newsroom close to the city agenda: roads, utilities, police, courts, schools, culture, sports, elections, city council, neighborhoods, ОСМД, КП, hospitals, festivals, accidents.
- Prefer Russian for the main article unless the user asks for Ukrainian. Keep Ukrainian names of institutions, legal formulations, quotes, or source fragments when they naturally appear.
- Keep the tone mostly informational, but allow restrained editorial color when the topic is absurd, scandalous, bureaucratic, or socially painful.
- Sound local and specific, not national or glossy. The reader should feel the newsroom knows the streets, officials, enterprises, and recurring city problems.
- Prioritize concrete details: dates, times, sums, addresses, names, positions, organization names, article numbers, counts, routes, districts, and consequences.

## Article Shape

Use this default structure:

1. Headline: concrete fact plus place, number, consequence, or tension.
2. Optional sublead: one sentence that gives the main practical context.
3. Lead paragraph: answer what happened and when/where in 1-2 sentences.
4. Attribution: identify who reported, confirmed, commented, or published the information.
5. Details: add chronology, money, legal grounds, participants, or technical explanation.
6. Quote or paraphrase: include a short official comment when available.
7. Context: connect to earlier reporting with "Напомним", "Как мы сообщали ранее", or "Отметим".
8. Close: current status, next step, appeal, list of addresses, schedule, or consequence for residents.

For short briefs, 3-5 compact paragraphs are enough. For investigations, explainers, interviews, or myth/fact pieces, use subheads and a longer sequence.

## Headlines

- Make headlines direct and searchable: `В Бердянске...`, `Бердянцев...`, `На Косе...`, `12 домов...`, `Подробности...`, `Почему...`.
- Use numbers early when they carry the news: sums, ages, percentages, dates, number of houses, teams, participants.
- Use mild intrigue for civic conflict: "что известно", "подробности", "почему", "может остаться", "снова", "уже завтра".
- Add media markers only when relevant: `(видео)`, `(фото)`, `(+ВИДЕО)`.
- Avoid literary headlines, puns that hide the fact, and abstract national framing.

## Paragraphs and Sentences

- Use short paragraphs, usually 1-3 sentences.
- Start many paragraphs with attribution or context markers: "По информации...", "Как сообщил...", "Как рассказала...", "По словам...", "На данный момент...", "Ранее мы рассказывали...".
- Mix concise news sentences with occasional longer explanatory sentences when describing regulations, tariffs, repairs, court cases, or utilities.
- Put one fact cluster per paragraph. Do not make dense magazine-style blocks.
- Use direct quotes with a dash and speaker attribution. Keep quotes practical and brief.

## Common Phrases

Use these patterns naturally, not mechanically:

- `Как мы сообщали ранее...`
- `Напомним,...`
- `Отметим,...`
- `По информации пресс-службы...`
- `Как рассказал/сообщил/отметил...`
- `На данный момент...`
- `По факту происшествия проводится расследование.`
- `Жители микрорайона...`
- `Бердянцы жалуются/просят/призывают/могут остаться...`
- `Редакция готова предоставить возможность для комментария...`

## Source Handling

- Attribute claims to specific sources: police, прокуратура, горисполком, КП, ОСМД, organizers, doctors, teachers, eyewitnesses, social networks, press service.
- If information is unconfirmed, say so plainly: "эта информация не подтвердилась", "официального подтверждения пока нет", "по словам очевидцев".
- When officials disagree with residents, present both positions in separate paragraphs.
- For legal/police items, include qualification only if supplied: article, part, code, sanction, status of investigation.
- For municipal-service items, explain the practical impact: who loses water/gas/electricity, when, where, why, and what residents should do.

## Tone by Story Type

- Breaking/local incident: concise, factual, cautious, update-friendly.
- City council/utilities: practical, numbers-heavy, source-attributed, with consequences for residents.
- Social media complaint: begin with what residents noticed, then seek or include official reaction.
- Culture/sports/school event: warmer, community-centered, with names, awards, emotions, and audience reaction.
- Scandal/politics: sharper wording is acceptable, but keep factual anchors and avoid unsupported accusations.
- Charity/social appeal: direct, human, with contact details and a clear call to action.

## Formatting

- Use plain article text, not marketing copy.
- Use lists for schedules, addresses, routes, house numbers, or official itemization.
- Keep author/photo credits only if the user provides them.
- Preserve official names and capitalization as provided, even when mixed Russian/Ukrainian text appears.
- Do not add SEO sections, hashtags, generic summaries, or "conclusion" labels.

## Quality Check

Before finishing, verify:

- The first two paragraphs contain the core news fact.
- Every strong claim has a source, quote, or attribution.
- The article names the local place and affected people.
- The tone is local-news practical, not bloggy, literary, or promotional.
- The piece is original and does not reuse source-article wording beyond generic news formulas.
